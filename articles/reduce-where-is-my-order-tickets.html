Cut "Where Is My Order?" Tickets With Branded Tracking

🚚 Shipping 5 min read Updated September 2026

"Where is my order?" is commonly the single largest support category for eCommerce stores, and it is almost entirely self-inflicted. Customers ask because nobody told them. The typical post-purchase experience is one confirmation email, then total silence for two weeks, then a package appears. In that silence the customer has no way to distinguish "in transit" from "you took my money and vanished" — so they email you, or worse, they open a dispute.

Every one of those tickets costs you support time on an order you already won. This workflow replaces the silence with a sequence.

The Workflow

  1. Measure your baseline first. Count what percentage of your support tickets over the last 60 days are delivery status questions. Write the number down. Without it you will not be able to tell whether any of this worked, and you will not know how much support time you just bought back.
  2. Map your current notification gaps. List every message a customer receives from checkout to delivery, with timing. Most stores discover they send exactly one email, at purchase. The gap between "order confirmed" and "delivered" is where all your tickets are generated — and it is usually the longest stretch of the entire customer relationship.
  3. Build the notification sequence. Fill the gap with four triggered messages: dispatched (with tracking), in transit, out for delivery, and delivered. Send them under your own branding rather than the carrier's. This matters more than it sounds — a carrier-branded email teaches customers that the relationship is with the carrier, while a branded one keeps you in the conversation and creates a legitimate reason to be in their inbox.
  4. Add a self-service returns portal. Returns are the other half of post-purchase support, and handling them over email is slow for everyone. A portal where the customer selects the item, picks a reason, and receives a label converts a multi-message thread into a form submission. It also gives you structured return-reason data, which tells you which products have a description or sizing problem.
  5. Re-measure after 30 days. Compare your delivery-question ticket percentage against the baseline from step one, and check repeat purchase rate while you are there. Branded post-purchase messaging tends to help both, because the customer has seen your name four more times in a context where you were being useful.
What this actually gets you A shorter support queue, fewer payment disputes filed out of uncertainty, and four additional branded touchpoints per order that you are not paying ad money to earn.
