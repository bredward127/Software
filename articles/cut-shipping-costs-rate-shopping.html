The Rate-Shopping Workflow That Cuts Your Parcel Costs

🚚 Shipping 5 min read Updated September 2026

For most stores, shipping is the second-largest variable cost after the product itself — and the one least often revisited. The typical pattern is that you picked a carrier when you launched, set a flat rate at checkout, and never looked again. Meanwhile carriers raise rates annually, your average parcel weight drifts upward as your catalog changes, and a slice of your orders quietly ship at a loss.

This workflow finds that slice and then makes the fix permanent, without raising your prices or renegotiating anything.

The Workflow

  1. Export 30 days of shipment data. You need five columns per order: billed weight, package dimensions, destination zone, what the carrier charged you, and what the customer paid at checkout. If you cannot produce this table, that is itself the finding — you are flying blind on a top-two cost line.
  2. Sort by margin, not by cost. Calculate charged minus paid for every order and sort ascending. The losses concentrate in a predictable pattern: heavy items, far zones, and oversized-but-light parcels where dimensional weight pricing kicks in. Now you know exactly which product/destination combinations are eating your margin.
  3. Connect a multi-carrier platform to get commercial rates. Individual sellers rarely ship enough volume to negotiate directly with carriers. Multi-carrier platforms aggregate the volume of all their merchants and pass discounted rates down, which is how a small store accesses pricing normally reserved for large shippers. Connect your store so live rates flow into your label workflow.
  4. Rate-shop at label creation, then turn it into rules. Comparing carriers manually per order does not survive a busy week. Convert your decisions into automation rules instead — for example: under one pound and domestic goes cheapest ground; anything with a two-day delivery promise goes to the specific service that honors it; oversized goes to whichever carrier prices dimensional weight most favorably. The system then picks correctly every time without you.
  5. Re-run the audit quarterly. Carriers publish general rate increases roughly annually, and your product mix shifts continuously. Repeating steps one and two every quarter takes about an hour and catches drift before it compounds into a full year of overpaying.
What this actually gets you Margin recovered from orders you are already fulfilling — the cheapest kind of profit there is, because it requires no additional traffic, no new products, and no price increase your customers will notice.
